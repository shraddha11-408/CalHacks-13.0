# IDD and employment: research backing for drigil

*Compiled 2026-10-24. Every number below has a source. Each line gives the data year. "Verified" means we read the figure on the primary source page. "Secondary" means we read it in a summary that cites the primary source, and someone should check it against the original before quoting it on stage.*

## 1. Headline stats (landing-page ready)

1. **Only 17% of adults with IDD who get state developmental-disability services have a paid community job.** (NCI-IDD In-Person Survey 2022-23, more than 25,000 people in 33 states. *Secondary.*) [NCI employment chapter](https://idd.nationalcoreindicators.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/IPS-22-23-Ch02-Employment_FINAL.pdf) · [summary](https://adasoutheast.org/national-core-indicators-intellectual-and-developmental-disabilities-in-person-survey-national-report-2022-23/)
2. **44% of those without a job say they want one.** (Same survey and caveat.) In the 2023-24 NCI data, nearly 3 people want to work for every 1 who does. ([NCI 2023-24 summary](https://www.hhs.nd.gov/sites/www/files/documents/Developmental%20Disabilities/23-24-nd-nci-idd-in-person-survey-at-a-glance.pdf), *secondary*)
3. **About 1 in 5 people in state IDD day and employment services get an integrated employment service.** In 2023 that was 22%: 138,990 of 646,601 people. (ICI StateData, cited by NJ CDD. *Secondary.*) [NJ CDD handout](https://njcdd.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/IDD-Individuals-Participation.pdf)
4. **In 2024, 22.7% of people with any disability were employed, against 65.5% of people without one.** (US BLS, CPS 2024. *Verified.* This counts all disabilities, not IDD alone.) [BLS TED](https://www.bls.gov/opub/ted/2025/22-7-percent-of-people-with-a-disability-were-employed-in-2024.htm)
5. **Direct support staff turn over at about 38–40% a year.** The 2023 weighted average was 39.7% (state range 23.6–48.6%), and the 2024 average was 37%. (NCI State of the Workforce. *Secondary for 2024.*) [2023 report](https://idd.nationalcoreindicators.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-NCI-IDD-SoTW_241126_FINAL.pdf) · [2024 at a glance](https://idd.nationalcoreindicators.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/SotW-IDD-2024-At-a-Glance.pdf)

7. **About 1% of people worldwide have an intellectual disability** (10.37 per 1,000, pooled from 52 studies). (Maulik et al., 2011.) [Res Dev Disabil 32(2):419–36](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2010.12.018)
8. **About 1 in 60 US children aged 3–17 has a diagnosed intellectual disability**: 1.70% (2019), 2.20% (2020), 1.65% (2021). (CDC/NCHS Data Brief 473, 2023. *Verified.*) [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db473.htm)
9. **Supported employment cost taxpayers $19,101 per person, against $45,684 in a sheltered workshop.** This compares the same people when they held each kind of job. (Cimera. *Secondary.*) [Cimera 2014 summary](https://mn.gov/mnddc/partnersinpolicymaking/Class35_materials/Cimera-CPSD-2014-Supported-Employment.pdf)

(Damianidou et al., 2018. *Verified.*) [PMC8115596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8115596/)

## 2. Findings by topic

### 2.1 What IDD is, and how common it is
- **Definition (AAIDD, 12th ed., 2021):** intellectual disability means significant limits in both intellectual functioning and adaptive behavior (conceptual, social and practical skills). It starts in the developmental period, which AAIDD now defines as before age 22. The 2002 edition used 18. [AAIDD definition](https://www.aaidd.org/intellectual-disability/definition) · [AAIDD FAQ](https://aaidd.org/intellectual-disability/faqs-on-intellectual-disability)
- **"Developmental disabilities"** is a wider umbrella that includes ID, autism and other conditions. "IDD" covers both.
- **US children:** 1.70% / 2.20% / 1.65% had diagnosed ID in 2019 / 2020 / 2021, with no significant change. Any developmental disability rose from 7.40% to 8.56%. ID prevalence rises with age. (CDC NCHS Data Brief 473, July 2023, NHIS.) [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/products/databriefs/db473.htm)
- **Global:** 10.37 per 1,000, about 1%. Prevalence is almost twice as high in low- and middle-income countries. (Maulik, Mascarenhas, Mathers, Dua & Saxena, 2011, 52 studies, 1980–2009.) [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ridd.2010.12.018)

### 2.2 Employment
- **All disabilities (BLS 2024):** 22.7% employed with a disability vs 65.5% without. For ages 16–64 it was 37.4%. Unemployment was 7.5% vs 3.8%. BLS does not report IDD separately. [BLS release, Feb 2025](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/archives/disabl_02252025.htm)
- **IDD service users (NCI 2022-23):** 17% in paid community jobs; 44% of those without one want one. (*Secondary; check the NCI PDF.*) [NCI employment chapter](https://idd.nationalcoreindicators.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/IPS-22-23-Ch02-Employment_FINAL.pdf)
- **Integrated employment services (ICI StateData, 2023):** 22% of people in state IDD day and employment services. Day programs and facility-based services still take most of the funding and most of the people. (*Secondary.*) [NJ CDD](https://njcdd.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/01/IDD-Individuals-Participation.pdf) · [ICI StateData](https://www.statedata.info/)
- **Wages:** sheltered-workshop wages averaged $1.36/hour in 2009. Since the 1980s, inflation-adjusted wages rose 17.4% for supported employees and fell 66.1% for sheltered workers. (Cimera 2014. *Secondary.*) [Cimera 2014](https://mn.gov/mnddc/partnersinpolicymaking/Class35_materials/Cimera-CPSD-2014-Supported-Employment.pdf)

### 2.3 Supported employment and job coaches
- **Model:** supported employment has four phases: a jobseeker profile, job development, **on-site training that fades over time**, and long-term follow-along supports (Wehman and colleagues, 2012, as described in secondary training material). Fading guides recommend starting to fade on day one, at a pace set by the worker's independence, while building coworker supports. [USU fading fact sheet](https://idrpp.usu.edu/cei/files/resources/fact-sheets/fading-job-coaching-supports.pdf) · [LSU job-site training](https://www.hdc.lsuhsc.edu/employment/docs/Core%20Job%20Site%20Training%20March%202022.pdf)
- **Cost:** in every state, supported employment was cost-efficient for taxpayers compared with sheltered work (Cimera, national VR data on 104,213 people with ID). Per-person service costs were $19,101 supported vs $45,684 sheltered. (*Secondary.*) [Cimera 2014](https://mn.gov/mnddc/partnersinpolicymaking/Class35_materials/Cimera-CPSD-2014-Supported-Employment.pdf)
- **Sheltered work is not a stepping stone.** People with no sheltered-work history are just as likely to get competitive integrated jobs, and no study has shown a cost-benefit for sheltered workshops (Taylor et al., 2021 scoping review, as summarized by UNT). (*Secondary.*) [UNT summary](https://disabilityinnovation.unt.edu/sites/default/files/what_to_know_-_research_on_competitive_integrated_and_sheltered_employment.pdf)
- **Workforce shortage:** DSP turnover was 39.7% in 2023 (weighted) and 37% in 2024. In 2024, 26% of agencies turned away referrals for lack of staff (38% in 2023). Job coaches come from this same strained workforce, so coach hours are scarce. [NCI SotW 2023](https://idd.nationalcoreindicators.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/2023-NCI-IDD-SoTW_241126_FINAL.pdf) · [SotW 2024](https://idd.nationalcoreindicators.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/SotW-IDD-2024-At-a-Glance.pdf)
- *Not found:* a verified national figure for job-coach caseloads or for typical hours per week after fading. Don't quote one.

### 2.4 Evidence for the instructional methods
- **Technology-based prompting:** Damianidou, Arthur-Kelly, Lyons & Wehmeyer (2018) updated Wehmeyer et al. (2006) with 41 single-subject studies (2004–2016). Applied cognitive technology improved employment-related outcomes. Effects differed by level of disability and by whether the technology used universal design features. [PMC8115596](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8115596/) · DOI [10.1080/20473869.2018.1439819](https://doi.org/10.1080/20473869.2018.1439819)
- **Self-prompting on handheld devices:** in single-case studies, young adults with moderate ID used PDAs with picture, audio and video prompts to self-prompt multi-step tasks, and to complete them on their own. (Primary studies, not a meta-analysis.) [Education and Training in Autism and DD, 2010](https://citedrive.com/en/discovery/evaluation-of-a-personal-digital-assistant-as-a-self-prompting-device-for-increasing-multi-step-task-completion-by-students-with-moderate-intellectual-disabilities/)
- **Most-to-least prompting:** Kutlu (2023) reviewed 19 single-case studies (2000–2021) of people with DD and found MLP effective across skills. In a separate study, MLP and constant time delay worked about equally well, but MLP took less training time, fewer trials and produced fewer errors (Aykut, 2012). [Kutlu 2023 record](https://search.trdizin.gov.tr/yayin/detay/1184077) · [Aykut 2012](https://avesis.gazi.edu.tr/yayin/450a600c-10d5-4a9d-88a8-15dd6bab9cdf/effectiveness-and-efficiency-of-constant-time-delay-and-most-to-least-prompt-procedures-in-teaching-daily-living-skills-to-children-with-intellectual-disabilities)
- **Video modeling and video prompting:** a meta-analysis of 20 single-case studies (67 participants with autism and/or ID) found video modeling effective for independent-living skills, and most likely to work when combined with prompting. (NASET/JAASEP 2019; effect size not quoted here.) [JAASEP review](https://www.naset.com/publications/jaasep-research-based-journal-in-special-education/jaasep-fall-2019/a-systematic-review-of-video-modeling-interventions-to-improve-the-independent-living-skills-of-students-with-autism-spectrum-disorder-and-intellectual-disability)
- *Unverified, don't quote:* pooled effect sizes for video prompting on vocational tasks, and a head-to-head meta-analysis of least-to-most vs most-to-least prompting. We could not open the full texts.

### 2.5 Natural supports (coworkers)
- **Scoping review (J. Vocational Rehabilitation, 2024; 17 articles, 1985–2024):** when coworkers supported employees with IDD, those employees engaged more in vocational tasks, interacted more with coworkers, and gained wages, hours and **job tenure**. [JVR-240017](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JVR-240017)
- **Mank, Cioffi & Yovanoff (1997–1999, AAMR):** more typical jobs and more coworker involvement went with better wage and integration outcomes, and jobs that started atypical tended to stay atypical. (*We saw the citation only, not the full text.*) [Mental Retardation 37(5):383](https://meridian.allenpress.com/idd/article-split/37/5/383/8132/Impact-of-Coworker-Involvement-With-Supported)
- **Job coaches** surveyed (n=202) agreed that coworker supports matter. The barriers they named were coworkers' lack of knowledge, the work environment, and employer indifference. [UW-Stout thesis](https://minds.wisconsin.edu/handle/1793/40493)

### 2.6 Why jobs are lost
- **Salzberg et al. (1988) review of 13 termination studies:** they sorted the reasons people lost jobs into job responsibility (attendance, punctuality), **task production (poor quality and rate of work)**, task-related social skills (not following instructions, poor response to criticism), and personal-social skills. [USU Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/sped_facpub/93)
- **South Carolina agency data (2001–02):** the most common reason for job loss was **job performance problems (28.1%)**, then the employee quitting (21.9%), health (10.9%) and layoffs (10.9%). (*Secondary abstract.*) [JVR 00334](https://content.iospress.com/articles/journal-of-vocational-rehabilitation/jvr00334)
- **Timing:** job loss happened most often within the first year (dissertation, 51 workers with severe disabilities; *secondary*). In a UK study, 13 of 49 supported jobs had broken down by follow-up (Banks et al., 2010). [Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/36470)

## 3. Method → evidence → drigil feature

| drigil feature | Method in the literature | Evidence (source) | Strength |
|---|---|---|---|
| Learns a task from one demo and breaks it into small steps | Task analysis, the base of systematic instruction | Fading and job-site training models in supported employment ([USU](https://idrpp.usu.edu/cei/files/resources/fact-sheets/fading-job-coaching-supports.pdf), [LSU](https://www.hdc.lsuhsc.edu/employment/docs/Core%20Job%20Site%20Training%20March%202022.pdf)); multi-step self-prompting studies ([PDA, 2010](https://citedrive.com/en/discovery/evaluation-of-a-personal-digital-assistant-as-a-self-prompting-device-for-increasing-multi-step-task-completion-by-students-with-moderate-intellectual-disabilities/)) | Practice standard; single-case evidence |
| Spoken step-by-step instructions on a wearable | Technology-aided self-prompting (audio/picture/video) | Damianidou et al. 2018 meta-analysis, 41 studies ([PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8115596/)); Wehmeyer et al. 2006 | Meta-analysis (single-case) |
| Instructions in a familiar coach's voice | Consistent prompts; video and audio models of the actual trainer | No direct study of a cloned voice found. **Don't claim evidence**; frame it as a design choice for continuity | Hypothesis |
| Camera checks each step and corrects gently, right away | Immediate feedback; error correction inside prompting procedures | MLP gives fewer errors and less training time ([Aykut 2012](https://avesis.gazi.edu.tr/yayin/450a600c-10d5-4a9d-88a8-15dd6bab9cdf/effectiveness-and-efficiency-of-constant-time-delay-and-most-to-least-prompt-procedures-in-teaching-daily-living-skills-to-children-with-intellectual-disabilities)); quality and rate errors are a leading reason for job loss ([Salzberg 1988](https://digitalcommons.usu.edu/sped_facpub/93)) | Single-case and review |
| Fades support per step (full → cue → alerts → independent) | Most-to-least prompting and prompt fading; fading of job-coach support | Kutlu 2023 MLP review ([record](https://search.trdizin.gov.tr/yayin/detay/1184077)); supported employment's phase of training that fades over time ([USU](https://idrpp.usu.edu/cei/files/resources/fact-sheets/fading-job-coaching-supports.pdf)) | Systematic review plus practice model |
| Routes help to a coworker buddy first, then the supervisor | Natural supports | 2024 JVR scoping review (tenure, wages, social interaction) ([JVR](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3233/JVR-240017)); Mank et al. coworker studies | Scoping review |
| Remembers each worker's past mistakes | Individualized, data-based instruction | Effects differ by level of disability ([Damianidou 2018](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8115596/)), so one script does not fit everyone | Indirect |
| Extends a scarce job coach (the "why now") | Workforce gap | DSP turnover of 37–40%; 26% of agencies turning away referrals ([NCI SotW](https://idd.nationalcoreindicators.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/SotW-IDD-2024-At-a-Glance.pdf)) | National survey |

## 4. Language guide

- **Default to person-first:** "people with intellectual and developmental disabilities," "a worker with IDD." Some people prefer identity-first language, so **use the person's own preference** and ask when you can. (The Arc and AAIDD joint position statement, rev. 2020.) [The Arc](https://thearc.org/position-statements/self-advocacy/) · [AUCD](https://www.aucd.org/template/page.cfm?id=605) · [Special Olympics](https://inclusivehealth.specialolympics.org/tools-resources/language-referring-to-people-with-IDD)
- **Never** use the r-word or "mentally retarded." Older studies use those terms; quote them only as citation titles. Avoid "suffers from," "high/low functioning," and "special needs worker."
- **Use "capable worker who uses supports," not "a burden on the coach."** The support is the product; the worker is not the problem.
- **Describe the support, not the person:** "Jordan uses step reminders," not "Jordan can't remember steps."
- **In the product:** never "wrong" or "no" alone. Say "Almost." plus the fix. One action per sentence, plain words. (Stepwise rule, AGENTS.md §2, which fits these guides.)
- **Respect autonomy:** the worker controls voice and recording consent, and the tool never makes discipline decisions.
